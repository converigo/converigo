# Content Quality Standard

## Purpose

This standard defines the editorial and SEO expectations for all Learning Center and SEO articles published on the Converigo platform. It is intended to ensure that content is useful, structurally consistent, search-intent aligned, and easy to maintain across the existing JSON-driven article system.

This document is design-only and should be used as a publishing and review reference. It reuses the existing article schema structure wherever possible.

---

## 1. Core Publishing Principles

All articles should be:
- accurate and factual
- easy to scan
- written for a clear search intent
- structured for both humans and search engines
- internally connected to relevant related content
- aligned with the existing article schema and SEO rendering pipeline

Articles should support one primary intent and one primary user outcome.

---

## 2. Article Structure

Every article should follow a consistent structure that maps cleanly to the existing article schema.

### Required Content Blocks

1. Title
   - Clear, specific, and search-intent aligned
   - Should communicate the topic directly

2. Description
   - A concise summary of the article purpose and value
   - Should help users decide quickly whether the article is relevant

3. Slug
   - Short, descriptive, keyword-friendly, and stable
   - Should follow the existing slug conventions

4. Category and topics
   - Category should reflect the article’s content family
   - Topics should capture the article’s primary keywords and related themes

5. Hero summary
   - A short introductory paragraph that explains what the reader will learn

6. Main body sections
   - Organized into clearly labeled sections
   - Each section should answer one question or support one idea

7. FAQ section
   - Includes search-driven questions commonly asked about the topic

8. Related content block
   - Links to relevant sibling articles, comparisons, converter pages, and format pages

9. CTA block
   - Encourages the next best action based on the article’s intent

### Recommended Section Pattern
- Intro / overview
- Key definition or explanation
- Practical guidance or comparison
- Common mistakes or considerations
- FAQ
- Related content and CTA

---

## 3. Heading Hierarchy

Headings should be clear, scannable, and logically structured.

### Required Hierarchy
- H1: one clear article title
- H2: main sections that divide the article into major topics
- H3: subpoints that support each H2 section

### Heading Rules
- Use one H1 only
- Avoid vague headings such as “More Info” or “Details”
- Make headings descriptive and intent-driven
- Use heading text that reflects the topic the reader is searching for
- Keep headings concise but meaningful

### Example
- H1: PNG vs JPG: Which Image Format Should You Use?
- H2: What is PNG?
- H2: What is JPG?
- H2: When should you choose PNG?
- H2: When should you choose JPG?
- H3: Transparency support
- H3: File size differences

---

## 4. Search Intent Guidelines

Each article must be written around a primary search intent.

### Intent Types

1. Informational intent
   - Explains a concept, comparison, or process
   - Examples: what is PNG, how does compression work

2. Transactional intent
   - Supports a direct action, such as converting a file or selecting a tool
   - Examples: convert JPG to PNG, choose the best converter

3. Commercial investigation intent
   - Helps the reader compare options before making a decision
   - Examples: PNG vs JPG, WebP vs PNG

### Guidelines
- Each article should focus on one dominant intent
- Avoid mixing too many unrelated intents in a single article
- Match the article’s structure to the intent
- Use search terms that align with the topic and content type

### Intent Alignment Examples
- Definition article: focus on explanation and clarity
- Comparison article: focus on decision support
- Guide article: focus on step-by-step usefulness
- Troubleshooting article: focus on issue resolution and clarity

---

## 5. Metadata Guidelines

Metadata should be concise, accurate, and aligned with the page purpose.

### Title Guidelines
- Keep titles clear and keyword-relevant
- Use the main topic early in the title
- Avoid stuffing multiple competing keywords
- Prefer natural phrasing over awkward SEO phrasing

### Description Guidelines
- Summarize the article in one clear sentence or two
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Communicate the benefit or takeaway
- Avoid duplication across multiple articles when possible

### SEO Keyword Guidelines
- Use a primary keyword for the main topic
- Add supporting keywords where natural
- Keep keyword usage natural and relevant
- Avoid keyword stuffing

### Schema and Structured Data Alignment
- Metadata should support the article type
- Article pages should align with the existing SEO rendering structure
- Canonical URLs should remain stable and accurate
- Breadcrumbs should reflect the article’s place in the content hierarchy

---

## 6. FAQ Guidelines

FAQ content should support the article’s core intent and improve search relevance.

### FAQ Requirements
- Include 3 to 6 high-value questions when appropriate
- Questions should reflect real user concerns and search phrasing
- Answers should be concise, direct, and informative
- FAQ content should not feel repetitive or promotional

### FAQ Quality Rules
- Each answer should add unique value
- Avoid generic filler questions
- Prefer practical, actionable questions over broad brand or product questions
- Keep the FAQ aligned with the article’s topic and audience

### FAQ Structure
Each FAQ item should include:
- question
- answer
- clear, readable language

This should remain compatible with the existing article schema FAQ structure.

---

## 7. CTA Guidelines

Every article should end with a clear next step.

### CTA Goals
- Guide the user toward the next relevant action
- Match the article’s intent
- Reinforce conversion or discovery behavior where useful

### CTA Types
- Conversion CTA: used for how-to and tool-related articles
- Comparison CTA: used for decision-oriented articles
- Education CTA: used for definition and overview articles
- Support CTA: used for troubleshooting or issue-resolution articles

### CTA Rules
- Keep the CTA specific and relevant
- Avoid vague phrases like “learn more” when a more concrete action exists
- Use the same terminology as the article’s central action
- Ensure the CTA supports the user journey rather than interrupting it

---

## 8. Internal Linking Rules

Internal linking is required to make content discoverable and semantically connected.

### Link Requirements
- Link to the relevant hub or category page where appropriate
- Link to at least one related sibling article
- Link to relevant converters or tools when the article has a practical or transactional purpose
- Link to related format or comparison content where helpful

### Link Quality Rules
- Links should be natural and helpful
- Avoid forced or repetitive linking
- Prefer links that deepen understanding or support the next action
- Keep link text descriptive and relevant

### Recommended Link Patterns
- Definition article -> related comparison article
- Comparison article -> related conversion article
- Guide article -> related troubleshooting or optimization article
- Hub article -> all major supporting articles

---

## 9. Content Quality Checklist

Use this checklist during drafting and review.

### Accuracy and clarity
- [ ] The article covers the topic accurately
- [ ] The main point is clear within the first paragraph
- [ ] The article avoids vague or unsupported claims

### Structure and readability
- [ ] The article has a clear H1
- [ ] Each section has a meaningful H2
- [ ] The article is easy to scan
- [ ] Paragraphs are concise and focused

### Search intent alignment
- [ ] The article targets one primary search intent
- [ ] The structure supports that intent
- [ ] The title, description, and sections are aligned

### SEO readiness
- [ ] The metadata is clear and relevant
- [ ] The article uses the primary keyword naturally
- [ ] FAQ content is useful and not repetitive
- [ ] Related links are present and meaningful

### Conversion and value
- [ ] The article includes a logical CTA
- [ ] The CTA matches the user’s likely next action
- [ ] The article helps the user make progress or reach a decision

---

## 10. Publication Checklist

Before publishing, verify the following:

1. The article matches the intended content type and search intent
2. The title and description are clear and relevant
3. The slug is clean and stable
4. The category and topics are set correctly
5. The article uses the required schema fields
6. The FAQ section is complete and useful
7. Related content links are included
8. The CTA is appropriate and action-oriented
9. Metadata and structured data are aligned with the page purpose
10. The content passes a final editorial review for clarity, usefulness, and consistency

---

## 11. Quality Scoring Rubric

Use the following rubric to assess article quality.

### 5 – Excellent
- Strong search intent alignment
- Clear structure and excellent readability
- High informational or practical value
- Strong metadata and SEO alignment
- Clear internal links and CTA
- Feels complete and well edited

### 4 – Strong
- Good intent alignment and structure
- Useful and mostly complete
- Minor issues in wording, linking, or depth
- Metadata and CTA are clear

### 3 – Acceptable
- Meets the basic requirements
- Some structure or clarity issues remain
- Limited depth or weak linking
- CTA is present but generic

### 2 – Needs Improvement
- Weak structure or unclear intent
- Sparse content or poor readability
- Metadata and internal linking are underdeveloped
- CTA does not meaningfully support the article

### 1 – Poor
- Lacks clarity, structure, or usefulness
- Does not meet the primary intent
- Missing or weak metadata, FAQ, linking, or CTA
- Not suitable for publication without revision

---

## 12. Review Standard

Articles should be reviewed for both editorial quality and SEO quality. A strong article should be:
- useful to the reader
- easy to understand
- clearly structured
- aligned with the target query
- connected to the broader content ecosystem

This standard should be applied consistently across Learning Center and SEO articles to maintain quality at scale.
